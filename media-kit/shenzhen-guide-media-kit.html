Two distinct audiences. The US is the largest by reach — travellers researching a China trip well in advance. Hong Kong and mainland China convert far better (around 1% CTR versus 0.13%), because those readers are days away from crossing a land border, not months.

Why they arrive

  • Crossing from Hong Kong to Shenzhen
  • Paying for things with a foreign card
  • Getting online without a VPN
  • Navigating the metro
  • Business travel and trade visits

Every one of these is a visitor with a departure date, solving a problem before they travel. It is high-intent, low-volume traffic, and connectivity sits at the front of the queue: it has to be handled before departure or not at all.

Why connectivity is the core topic

Visitors to mainland China arrive to find Google Maps, WhatsApp, Instagram and Gmail blocked at the network level. An international eSIM is the standard fix — and critically, it generally cannot be installed once you are already inside the country, because the providers' own sites are blocked.

That makes this a decision readers must make before they travel, while they are still reading guides like this one. The published eSIM guide is one of the site's most-read pages and currently lists Nomad as the strongest option for China.

Editorial standards

  • Every guide written by the site owner, a Shenzhen native
  • Commercial links disclosed sitewide and marked rel="sponsored"
  • Claims that have not been personally verified are labelled as such, in the text
  • Unconfirmed facts are flagged with a date rather than guessed at

The eSIM guide currently opens by stating plainly that these products have not been personally tested. That is the gap this partnership would close.

The November 2026 window

Shenzhen hosts the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on 18–19 November 2026, alongside the APEC CEO Summit. Around a thousand delegates plus a much larger wave of associated business travel will arrive in a city most of them have never visited. A dedicated APEC content cluster is already published and indexed, three months ahead of the event, aimed squarely at inbound business visitors — a group that needs working mobile data from the moment they land, and cannot obtain it after arrival.

What I'm asking for

A creator partnership including a test plan for a Hong Kong → Shenzhen crossing. I would publish a first-hand write-up of how it actually performs — disclosed as a provided unit, and honest about the result either way, including indoor coverage, which published testing suggests is the weak point.

I have no rate card and am not seeking a paid placement. I'd rather be able to write "I used this" than keep summarising other people's reviews.
