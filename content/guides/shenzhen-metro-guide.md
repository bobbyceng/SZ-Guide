The Shenzhen metro is fast, cheap, clean, and straightforward once you know the key lines. The network covers most destinations a visitor needs, and signs and announcements are in English throughout.

The one thing that confuses most foreigners is payment. This guide handles that first, then everything else.

> **APEC transport update · 15 October 2026:** Shenzhen has announced half-price metro and bus fares during the APEC meeting period. The announcement does not give the exact discount dates or payment conditions. This is a forthcoming event policy; do not assume it applies to travel today. [Read what is confirmed and what still needs checking](#guide-apec-2026-will-shenzhen-metro-and-buses-be-half-price).

## Payment: Your Options

### Option 1: Contactless Bank Card (Easiest)

Shenzhen metro takes contactless foreign cards directly. Tap at the entry gate, tap again at exit, and the fare is worked out by distance, typically ¥2–10.

**Which cards.** The reader accepts more than most guides list — **Visa, Mastercard, American Express and JCB**, alongside the domestic options (UnionPay, China T-Union, and digital yuan / e-CNY). If you have been told Amex won't work on the metro, that is out of date.

**All gates now accept international cards.** The operator’s [22 September 2026 announcement](https://www.szmc.net/home/xinwenzhongxin/gongsixinwen/202609/106228.html) says the upgrade took effect on **10 September**, across all gate channels. The earlier trial required a designated reader; that restriction is outdated. Your card needs contactless payment and small-value PIN-free payments enabled.

Tap in and out with **the same card or mobile payment device**. Do not switch from a physical card to its phone-wallet version midway through a journey. If the gate declines your tap, ask station staff or use a backup payment method.

A supported contactless card lets you ride without a metro app, a stored-value top-up or a local SIM. Your own bank may charge for foreign-currency transactions; check its terms. This guide does not assume a universal metro surcharge.

### Option 2: Alipay or WeChat Pay

Both apps support foreign credit cards and have metro payment built in. Open the metro mini-program inside either app (or scan the QR code reader at the turnstile), and scan at the gate.

Setup takes 10–15 minutes but is useful if you plan to use these apps for other payments during your trip, restaurants, shops, and street food vendors all accept them. Our [Alipay and WeChat Pay setup guide](/guides/alipay-wechat-pay-setup) walks through linking a foreign card, and it's worth doing before you arrive rather than at a turnstile.

### Option 3: Shenzhen Tong Card (深圳通) — mostly obsolete

The local transit card, sold at service counters for ¥30 (¥20 refundable deposit plus ¥10 credit). It still works on metro, buses and some taxis.

**But hardly anyone uses one any more.** Locals pay with a QR code in WeChat or Alipay, or tap their phone over NFC. You will not see many physical cards at the gates. There is no real reason for a visitor to buy one.

If you have already bought one, the ¥20 deposit is refundable, but **not at ordinary sales points** — only at Shenzhen Tong's own service centres, the metro service desks, self-service refund machines, or a post office branch. Undamaged cards get the full deposit back; a bent, cracked or defaced card loses ¥15 to a replacement fee. A passport is accepted as ID if the balance is large enough to require one.

### Option 4: Single-Journey Token

Vending machines at every station sell single-journey tokens for cash. Select your destination on the map (English language option available), pay, collect the token. Fine for one or two trips, inefficient for daily use.

## Is There a Security Check?

Yes, at every station, every time. This surprises almost every first-time visitor, and it is the single biggest difference from a metro in Europe or North America.

**What happens:** every bag goes through an X-ray scanner, and since **20 July 2026** each passenger is also scanned with a handheld detector. It is the same routine as boarding a Chinese high-speed train, slightly more relaxed. On a normal day it costs you a minute or two.

**The liquid rule catches people out.** If you are carrying an open drink, staff will usually ask you to **take a sip in front of them**, or run the bottle through a separate scanner. This is routine, not suspicion. A sealed bottle of water is fine.

**Limits worth knowing:**

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| **Power banks** | **20,000 mAh per unit, 5 units maximum** |
| Perfume, cologne, hairspray | 700ml per item, 1,000ml total |
| Alcohol (24–70%) | 3,000ml total |
| Prohibited outright | Flammables, controlled knives, live animals (guide dogs excepted), inflated balloons |

**The power bank limit matters more than it sounds.** If you have just bought power banks in [Huaqiangbei](/guides/huaqiangbei-electronics-guide) — which a lot of visitors do — check the capacity printed on them before you head for the metro.

**On queues:** the July 2026 upgrade caused serious backups in its first days, with waits at busy stations running 15–30 minutes longer than usual. The operator responded within 48 hours by opening all lanes, separating passengers without bags, and piloting walk-through scanners at Futian and Nanshan Book City. It has largely settled since. Allow a couple of extra minutes at rush hour and you will be fine.

## The Lines That Matter for Tourists

Start with the lines connecting your arrival point, hotel and planned stops.

Line numbers below are shown in their real colour, matching the station signage and the network map, so you can match what you read here to what you are standing in front of.

### Line 1, the Luobao Line

Runs east-west across the city. The most useful line for tourists coming from the Lo Wu border crossing.

Key stops: **Luohu** (Lo Wu border) → Laojie (old city center) → Huaqianglu → **Window of the World** → Shenzhen University

### Line 4, the Longhua Line

Runs north-south through the central business district. Connects the Futian Port border crossing with the city center.

Key stops: **Futian Port** → Convention & Exhibition Center → Civic Center → Children's Palace → **Shenzhen North** (high-speed rail) → Longhua

### Line 2 and Line 7

Both serve **Huaqiangbei station (华强北)**, the electronics shopping district.

> Careful here: **Line 1 does not stop at Huaqiangbei.** Line 1 has **Huaqiang Road (华强路)**, a different station about 400m away. If your app routes you via Line 1, you are going to the other one — still walkable, just not where you expected. See the [Huaqiangbei guide](/guides/huaqiangbei-electronics-guide) for which buildings are worth your time once you're there.

### Line 11, Airport Express

From **Shenzhen Bao'an Airport** into the city, about 30 minutes to Futian. It does **not** stop at Airport East — that is the end of Line 1, a separate station some distance from the terminal. Line 11 joins the rest of the network at Qianhai Bay (Lines 1 and 5), Houhai (Lines 2 and 13), Chegongmiao (Lines 1, 7 and 9), Futian (Lines 2 and 3) and Gangxia North (Lines 2, 10 and 14).

It also runs later than most lines: the last train from the airport into the city leaves at about **00:02**. The [airport-to-city guide](/guides/shenzhen-airport-to-city) walks through the whole trip, including where to find the business carriage on the platform.

> **Line 11 has a business-class carriage, and almost no visitor knows about it.**
>
> This is the one line where it exists, and it is the line you are most likely to be riding with luggage. The catch is how you board: after you tap through the normal gate, the business-class section has **a second barrier of its own**, and you tap again there — card or QR code — to get in. It costs more than a standard fare.
>
> If you are coming from the airport with suitcases during rush hour, it is worth it. If you walk onto the business carriage without doing the second tap, that is fare evasion rather than a lucky upgrade.

### Line 2 / Line 8

Runs along the northern side of Futian. Easy to overlook, but it's the line that serves **Xiangmi Lake**, where the [APEC 2026 venue](/guides/apec-2026-shenzhen-visitor-guide) sits. Note that **Xiangmi station (Line 2/8) and Xiangmihu station (Line 1) are different places** despite the near-identical names, which catches people out.

## How to Navigate

**Best app: Amap (高德地图).** Available in English, works without a VPN, gives real-time metro directions including which car number to board for the fastest exit. Download before you arrive.

Google Maps works for basic metro routing in Shenzhen but doesn't show real-time information.

**For an offline map**, the Shenzhen Metro official app has an English interface, and TravelChinaGuide publishes a [downloadable English network map](https://www.travelchinaguide.com/images/map/guangdong/shenzhen-subway.pdf). Worth having on your phone before you land, since you will want it in the station where signal is patchy.

**If you lose something**, the official app takes lost-property reports in English. There is no English phone line — the general hotline (0755-88960600) is Mandarin — but the ticket machines and service kiosks now run a three-language assistant covering English.

At the station, follow the English signs, they're clear throughout the system. Platform announcements are in Mandarin and English. The direction of travel is shown by the terminal station name (e.g., "towards Luohu" or "towards Airport").

## Fares and Hours

### APEC 2026: will Shenzhen Metro and buses be half price?

**Yes, a 50% fare discount has been announced for the APEC meeting period.** [Guangdong Release, the Guangdong provincial government information office’s official account](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-10-15/detail-inivhpns4617013.d.html?his=0&pos=108&vt=4), reported the announcement on **15 October 2026**, following Shenzhen’s transport press conference that morning.

**The exact start and end dates are not specified in that announcement.** Do not assume the discount is already active or use the Leaders’ Meeting dates as the full discount window. The **16–20 November** dates mentioned in reporting refer to a [flexible-working initiative](https://www.yicai.com/news/103394711.html), not a confirmed fare-discount timetable.

**Payment and eligibility details still need checking.** The announcement does not explain how the discount will work for foreign contactless cards, QR codes or single-journey tickets, or whether Line 11 business-class travel is included. It also does not establish passenger eligibility or whether the discount combines with other concessions. Before relying on a discounted fare, check Shenzhen Metro’s current notices or ask station staff which fare applies to your payment method and journey.

[Additional metro capacity and bus–metro connections](https://www.sztv.com.cn/ysz/zx/zw/83907987.shtml) have also been announced. This does not guarantee a two-minute service on every line or an unchanged route during event-week traffic controls. Follow current operator notices for your journey.

### Regular fares and operating hours

The figures below describe regular travel, not a confirmed APEC discounted tariff.

**Fares:** ¥2–14 depending on distance. A typical cross-city journey (Luohu to Window of the World) costs around ¥6–8.

**Hours:** roughly **6:30am to 11:00pm**, with small variations by line. Check the posted schedule at your departure station if you are travelling late — services are occasionally extended on public holidays.

## Tips

**Check the exit number before you leave the platform.** Large stations (Civic Center, Shenzhen North, Window of the World) have 10+ exits, each leading to a different street-level location. The right exit number is in Amap directions, note it before you go through the gate.

**Rush hour is worse than "less comfortable."** 7:30–9:00am and 6:00–8:00pm on weekdays, **you should expect to let one or two trains go before you can physically get on.** Lines 1, 5 and 11 are the worst of it. This is not a queue-politely situation, it is a wait-your-turn-to-be-compressed situation, and it is entirely normal here.

**Some interchanges are long walks, not cross-platform hops.** **Chegongmiao (车公庙), Shenzhen North (深圳北), Gangsha North (岗厦北), Buji (布吉) and Xili (西丽)** are large stations where a "change here" on your app can mean five to ten minutes of walking. Budget for it when you are timing a train or a flight.

**Luggage is fine.** Suitcases go through the gates without trouble, some stations have wider gates for exactly this, and there are lifts at street level. The bags still go through the X-ray scanner like everything else.

**Plan your connection for navigation.** Check whether your existing roaming plan includes mainland China. If it does, you may not need another SIM. Otherwise, set up a [travel eSIM](/guides/best-esim-for-shenzhen) before arrival, and download a network map as a backup for stations with weak signal.

I have not personally tested Saily; the [eSIM guide](/guides/best-esim-for-shenzhen) compares other options.

**The metro doesn't cover every destination.** Some restaurants, smaller hotels, and street-level areas are a 10–15 minute walk from the nearest station. Combining metro for main journeys with [DiDi](/guides/didi-shenzhen-guide) for the last mile is the most practical approach.

**Follow the posted rules and staff instructions.** Keep food packed away on trains and leave no litter.

**Pick your hotel by metro line, not by district.** Shenzhen districts are enormous, and "Futian" spans stations 30 minutes apart. [Where to stay in Shenzhen](/guides/where-to-stay-in-shenzhen) breaks this down by area.

## The Bottom Line

The Shenzhen metro is one of the better urban transit systems in Asia, well-maintained, extensive, and genuinely foreigner-friendly. Use a contactless Visa or Mastercard if you have one and you won't need to think about payment at all.

Once you know the 4–5 lines and stations relevant to where you're going, navigating feels straightforward. The learning curve is about 20 minutes on your first day.
