Here is the mistake I expect a lot of people to make in November.

Shenzhen has a metro station called **Xiangmihu** and a different metro station called **Xiangmi**. They are on different lines. They are not next to each other. And the APEC venue is walkable from one of them and not the other.

If you're booking a hotel for the week of **November 18-19, 2026**, that distinction matters more than which district you pick. This guide covers where to base yourself, what each area is actually like, and the booking traps that catch foreign visitors in China.

It also works as a general answer to a question the usual "where to stay in Shenzhen" advice doesn't handle: **how do you choose a hotel when you're here for one specific place, rather than for sightseeing?**

## The Trap: Two Stations, Nearly the Same Name

| Station | Line | Where it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **Xiangmi (香蜜)** | **Line 2 / Line 8** | Qiaoxiang Rd × Xiangmihu Rd — **the venue's northwest corner** |
| **Xiangmihu (香蜜湖)** | **Line 1** | Shennan Blvd × Xiangmei Rd — south of the venue, by the golf course |

The APEC main venue is the **Shenzhen International Exchange Center** (深圳国际交流中心), on the **north** side of Xiangmi Lake. Its site is bounded by Qiaoxiang Road to the north and Xiangmihu Road to the west — which is exactly where **Xiangmi station on Line 2/8** sits.

**Xiangmihu station on Line 1** sounds like the obvious one. It isn't. It's on Shennan Boulevard, on the far side of the lake district, and you'd be adding a long walk or a taxi.

> **The practical rule:** for this venue, you want **Line 2 or Line 8**. Book by metro line, not by district name. "Futian" is the size of a small city and includes both of these stations.

And while we're clearing things up: the venue is **not** the Shenzhen Convention and Exhibition Center in the CBD near Shopping Park. That's a different building about fifteen minutes away, and even locals mix these up. Full background is in the [APEC 2026 visitor guide](/guides/apec-2026-shenzhen-visitor-guide).

## Quick Answer

| You are... | Stay | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Attending something at the venue | **Line 2/8 corridor** (Xiangmi, Xiangmei North, Jingtian) | Closest, quietest, thinnest on food |
| Here for business, want food and life nearby | **Futian CBD** (Shopping Park / Convention Center) | Best density, short hop to the venue |
| Want distance from event congestion | **Nanshan** (Houhai / Science Park) | Newer, calmer, costs more |
| Want the most international atmosphere | **Shekou / Sea World** | Expat heart of the city, but far |
| On a budget, or commuting to Hong Kong | **Luohu** or **Futian Checkpoint** | Cheapest, right on the border |

## The Venue Area Itself: Quiet, and Thin on Food

I don't spend much time in Xiangmi Lake, and that's telling in itself. It's **old-money Shenzhen** — the closest thing this city has to an established wealthy district. Low density, big residential compounds, the Shenzhen Golf Club, and the lake and park at the middle of it. It is genuinely pleasant and genuinely quiet.

What it is not is a place with street life. **There is very little walkable commercial around the venue.** The one cluster that used to serve the area, a dining and culture strip called Xiangmi Lake 1979, was demolished in 2025 to make way for this exact venue and the new metro line. The nearest real shopping and dining anchor now is around **Nonglin Road** — the InCity mall there has a Sam's Club and a normal range of restaurants.

**What this means for you:** if you're attending the event, catering is handled on site and a quiet hotel nearby is a fine choice. If you're a visitor who wants to walk out of your hotel and find dinner, **don't base yourself here** — go one or two stops toward Chegongmiao or the CBD, where the choice multiplies.

> The upside of the area is real, though. Xiangmi Park has just been renovated and the lake refilled. If you're here for meetings and want thirty minutes of not-a-city, it's right there.

## Futian CBD: The Default for Most Business Visitors

This is where I'd tell most people to stay, including most people coming for APEC week.

The CBD around **Shopping Park** and **Convention Center** stations gives you the density that Xiangmi Lake doesn't: Shopping Park, COCO Park, KingGlory Plaza and the Liancheng New Times underground street are all connected to each other, so your evenings and your lunches solve themselves without a plan.

- **Nightlife:** the open-air bar strip by Shopping Park station is Futian's main after-hours cluster, and it draws a visible international crowd. It's the closest thing central Shenzhen has to a going-out district.
- **Liancheng New Times** is worth knowing about specifically: it's an underground retail street linking several CBD stations. In heat or rain you can move between stations, eat, and shop without surfacing. Shenzhen weather makes this more useful than it sounds.
- **Getting to the venue:** you're on the Line 1 / Line 3 corridor here, so you'll change to Line 2/8 rather than ride direct. Still short — this is a cross-town hop, not a journey.
- **Trade-off:** it's a CBD, and hotels on Shennan Boulevard get traffic noise. Book something set back from the main road.

This is also where prices will move most that week, and where availability will disappear first.

## Nanshan: Newer, Calmer, More Expensive

Nanshan is the tech district — Tencent, DJI, the startup scene. The **Houhai** and **Science Park** areas are the practical business base: newer hotel stock, good food, and the Shenzhen Bay coastal park nearby, which is one of the genuinely nice things about this city.

Shopping splits into two very different places, and people conflate them:

- **MixC World (万象天地)** is an open-air street-format mall. Younger, busier, lots of first-in-Shenzhen brands, food running late. Go here if you want atmosphere.
- **Qianhai MixC (万象前海)** is the quieter, more design-led, upmarket one. Better for a business dinner, worse for people-watching.

**Cost:** expect Nanshan to run meaningfully above Futian for equivalent quality — call it 20-30% for a comparable business hotel, more at the top end.

**The APEC-week argument for Nanshan:** you're well clear of any traffic management around Xiangmi Lake, and the Line 11 express gets you to the airport in about half an hour. If your schedule that week is meetings around the city rather than anything at the venue, this is the calmer call.

## Shekou and Sea World: The Most International Corner

If you want the part of Shenzhen that feels least like a business trip, this is it.

**Shekou** is the old expat neighbourhood in western Nanshan, and **Sea World** is its centre — a waterfront plaza built around a permanently moored ship, ringed by Western restaurants, bars, international supermarkets and international schools. The foreign population here is visibly higher than anywhere else in the city.

I go out there to walk, and I'd recommend it to anyone with a free evening. It's one of the few places in Shenzhen where the pace drops.

**The catch is distance.** Shekou to the Xiangmi Lake venue is a long ride — comfortably 40+ minutes on the metro. As a base for APEC week it only makes sense if the event isn't actually your reason for being here. As a place to spend one evening, it's the best answer on this page.

## Luohu and Futian Checkpoint: Budget and Border

**Luohu** is the oldest district, right at the Lo Wu border crossing. It's the cheapest area with real hotel stock, and it's the obvious base if you're moving back and forth to Hong Kong. It's also visibly older than Futian and thinner on international-standard hotels — and it's where the foreigner-acceptance problem below bites hardest.

**Futian Checkpoint** is the underrated middle option: cheaper than the CBD, direct rail link into Hong Kong, and a reasonable metro ride to the venue area. If you're arriving from Hong Kong and price matters, look here before Luohu.

## Booking: Two Things That Are Different in China

**1. Not every hotel can legally take you.**

Chinese hotels need a specific police registration to host foreign guests. In late 2024 three ministries — Public Security, Commerce, and the National Immigration Administration — jointly instructed hotels to stop turning foreign guests away, and the direction of travel is clearly toward this being a non-issue. But **enforcement is uneven**, and budget properties, guesthouses and some smaller domestic chains still can't process a foreign passport at the front desk. The failure mode is being refused at 11pm with your luggage.

Stick to international brands or established 4-5 star Chinese hotels, book through an international platform, and if a listing is cheap and unfamiliar, confirm before you pay. The [where to stay guide](/guides/where-to-stay-in-shenzhen) covers the checks in detail.

**2. Book earlier than you would elsewhere.**

Shenzhen normally has deep hotel inventory and stable prices. **The week of November 16-20, 2026 is the exception.** Delegations will block out CBD and venue-area stock, roughly a thousand delegates are expected for the Leaders' Meeting alone, and associated events run either side of it. Expect noticeably higher rates and real scarcity near the venue.

> **Book by early October at the latest**, and take the free-cancellation rate. You lose nothing by holding a room and rebooking if something better appears.

**3. Payment and check-in.** Bring your physical passport — a photo is not accepted anywhere in China. Prepay online where you can, since front desks can't always process a foreign card in person. Setting up [Alipay or Weixin Pay](/guides/alipay-wechat-pay-setup) before you land solves most of this.

## Common Questions

**Which metro station is the APEC 2026 venue closest to?**
Xiangmi (香蜜) on Line 2 / Line 8. Not Xiangmihu (香蜜湖) on Line 1, despite the name — that one is on Shennan Boulevard, on the other side of the lake district.

**Is the APEC venue the Shenzhen Convention and Exhibition Center?**
No. It's the purpose-built Shenzhen International Exchange Center at Xiangmi Lake. The Convention and Exhibition Center is a different building in the Futian CBD, roughly fifteen minutes away.

**Should I stay right next to the venue?**
Only if you're attending something there. The area is quiet and residential with very little walkable dining. Most visitors are better off in the Futian CBD and riding over.

**When should I book for APEC week?**
Early October at the latest, with free cancellation. Venue-area and CBD stock will go first.

**Can I attend APEC 2026?**
The Leaders' Meeting isn't open to the public, and the associated business programmes have their own registration. Most people in Shenzhen that week are here for other reasons and just need to plan around it.

**Where do foreigners actually hang out in Shenzhen?**
Shekou and Sea World for the highest concentration, and the bar strip by Shopping Park station in Futian for the central option.

**Will my hotel accept a foreign passport?**
International brands and established 4-5 star hotels, yes. Budget and independent properties, not reliably, despite the 2024 directive telling them to. Check before you book.
