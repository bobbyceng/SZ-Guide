Shenzhen hosts the **33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18-19, 2026**. It's the third time a Chinese city has hosted, after Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014, and the first time for Shenzhen.

If you're coming for the meeting, the CEO Summit, one of the ministerial meetings, or just doing business here during that window, this page is the practical layer: what you need to enter, how to get in, how to pay for things, and where to base yourself. I live here and write the rest of this site, so everything below links to a full guide if you need the detail.

> **A note on what's confirmed:** dates and host city are official. The exact venue and any special entry arrangements for attendees had not been publicly confirmed at the time of writing (August 2026). I've flagged those clearly rather than guessing. Check the official [APEC China 2026](https://www.apec.org/) and Shenzhen government channels for the final logistics, and check back here as details firm up.

## Do You Need a Visa?

There are four separate routes into mainland China, and which one applies to you depends on your passport and your itinerary. Check these in order.

### 1. Visa-free entry (most likely)

As of 2026, citizens of a large group of countries can enter mainland China visa-free for 30 days, including the US, UK, most of Europe, Australia, Canada, Japan, and South Korea. Visa-free arrivals now make up about **59% of all foreign inbound visitors** to Shenzhen, so this is the normal path, not the exception.

Search for the current list and confirm your nationality. The policy has been expanding steadily, so anything you read more than a few months old may understate what you're entitled to.

### 2. APEC Business Travel Card (ABTC)

If you hold a valid ABTC, you can stay in China for **up to 60 days**. If you're a regular business traveler across APEC economies and don't already have one, it's worth checking whether your economy issues them, though application lead times mean this is a decision for future trips rather than a fix for November.

### 3. 240-hour visa-free transit

If you're transiting between two other international destinations, Guangdong is one of the provinces covered by China's **240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit**, through multiple entry ports. This suits attendees who are combining APEC with stops elsewhere in Asia.

[Full 240-hour transit guide →](/guides/china-240-hour-visa-free-transit) covers who qualifies, what to show at the border, and the mistakes that get people turned back.

### 4. Shenzhen 5-day Special Economic Zone visa on arrival

Shenzhen has a short-stay visa-on-arrival option for some travelers who don't qualify for the routes above. It's restrictive and limited to Shenzhen itself, so treat it as a fallback, not a plan.

> **Don't leave this to the last week.** If none of the above covers you, you need an L or M visa from a Chinese embassy or consulate, and processing time in the run-up to a summit is not something you want to gamble on.

## Getting to Shenzhen

Two realistic routes, and which one is better depends on where you're flying from.

### Via Hong Kong (often the better connection)

Hong Kong has far more long-haul flights than Shenzhen, and the border is 45 minutes away. From Hong Kong you can either walk across at a land crossing or take the high-speed rail from West Kowloon to Shenzhen North in **14 minutes**.

For a business trip with luggage in November, the high-speed rail is worth the extra cost over a land crossing. It's covered, it's fast, and you skip the walking crossing entirely.

[Hong Kong to Shenzhen: every crossing compared →](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen) has costs, opening hours, which crossing puts you closest to Futian, and what actually happens at immigration step by step.

### Direct to Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport (SZX)

Bao'an has good coverage across Asia and a growing number of long-haul routes. It connects to the city by metro Line 11, which reaches Futian in about 35 minutes.

If you're coming from within Asia, flying direct is usually simpler. From Europe or the Americas, check Hong Kong first.

## Before You Land: The Three-Thing Checklist

This is the part visitors consistently get wrong, and it's the difference between a smooth first day and a frustrating one. Do all three **before you arrive**, while you still have unrestricted internet.

**1. eSIM.** Chinese domestic networks block Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, and most Western services. An international eSIM routes your traffic outside China so those keep working without a VPN. Install it before you leave. [eSIM guide →](/guides/best-esim-for-shenzhen)

**2. Payment app.** Link an international Visa or Mastercard to Alipay or Weixin Pay. No Chinese bank account needed. Verification can take up to 24 hours, so don't do this at the airport. [Payment setup guide →](/guides/alipay-wechat-pay-setup)

**3. DiDi.** China's ride-hailing app. Street-hailing a taxi in Shenzhen is unreliable and most drivers won't speak English, so DiDi with its in-app translation is how you actually get around outside the metro. [DiDi guide →](/guides/didi-shenzhen-guide)

## Paying for Things: Better Than You've Heard

The "China is cashless and foreigners can't pay for anything" story is out of date, and Shenzhen has been visibly upgrading this in the run-up to APEC. As of mid-2026:

- **41,000 merchants** across the city accept overseas bank cards, through roughly **51,000 POS terminals**, covering the airport, border checkpoints, major commercial districts, hotels, and tourist sites.
- **More than 40 overseas e-wallets** now work for QR payments in Shenzhen, including PayPal, Singapore's LiquidPay, and Thailand's K PLUS. If you already use one of these at home, you may be able to pay here without setting anything else up.
- The central bank's Shenzhen branch worked with Weixin Pay to simplify **card-linking for around 7 million overseas users** from more than 200 countries and regions.
- **Over 3,600 ATMs** in the city accept overseas-issued cards.

**What this means in practice:** in Futian and around the airport, a foreign credit card will physically work in more places than it used to. But QR payment is still the default everywhere else, and small vendors, older shops, and street food will still expect Alipay or Weixin. Set up a payment app anyway.

> **Still carry ¥200-300 in cash.** Not because you'll usually need it, but because the one time a terminal or an app fails, it's at the moment you're in a hurry.

## Where to Base Yourself

**Futian** is the host district and the obvious answer for anyone attending. It's the CBD, it has the international hotel stock, and it puts you closest to whatever the venue turns out to be. It's also where prices will rise most and availability will disappear first.

**Nanshan** is the alternative worth considering: the tech district, about 20-30 minutes from Futian by metro, generally newer hotels, and less likely to be caught inside any event traffic management. If your schedule has you in meetings rather than at the summit itself, this can be the calmer base.

**Luohu** is the budget option and sits right on the Lo Wu border crossing, which is useful if you're going back and forth to Hong Kong.

## Common Questions

**When exactly is APEC 2026 in Shenzhen?**
The Economic Leaders' Meeting is November 18-19, 2026. Associated meetings including the CEO Summit and the joint ministerial meeting run around the same period, and APEC-related events have been happening across China throughout 2026.

**Do I need a visa to attend APEC in Shenzhen?**
Most likely not, if you hold a passport from one of the countries covered by China's 30-day visa-free policy. ABTC holders get up to 60 days. Otherwise check the 240-hour transit route, or apply for a visa well in advance.

**Can I use my credit card in Shenzhen?**
In more places than you'd expect, especially in Futian, at the airport, and in hotels: about 41,000 merchants now accept overseas cards. But mobile payment is still the default, so set up Alipay or Weixin Pay with your foreign card before you arrive.

**How do I get from Hong Kong to Shenzhen for APEC?**
High-speed rail from West Kowloon to Shenzhen North takes 14 minutes, or you can cross on foot at Lo Wu or Lok Ma Chau/Futian. The Futian crossing puts you directly in the CBD. See the [full border crossing guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen).

**Where should I stay for APEC?**
Futian if you're attending, Nanshan if you want to avoid event congestion, Luohu on a budget or if you're commuting to Hong Kong. Book before October: CBD hotel availability in that week will go early.

**Will Google and WhatsApp work?**
Not on a Chinese domestic network. Get an international eSIM before you arrive and they'll work normally, no VPN needed.

**Is Shenzhen easy to get around without Chinese?**
The metro is bilingual and straightforward. Outside it, DiDi's in-app translation handles drivers, and payment apps remove most transactional conversation. You'll manage.
