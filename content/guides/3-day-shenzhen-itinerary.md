When a friend visits Shenzhen, I don't take them to the places at the top of most lists. I take them to the neighborhoods I actually spend time in. This guide is built from those.

A reality check first: Shenzhen is not a sightseeing city in the traditional sense. There is no Forbidden City here. What it does well is shopping, eating, and some genuinely pleasant places to walk and cycle by the water. Plan for that and you'll have a good time.

One more thing before the plans: I only name neighborhoods and big landmarks here, not individual restaurants or cafés. Shops open and close too fast for me to keep a list honest. Every area below has more than enough places to eat; pick whatever looks busy.

## Before You Arrive: The Setup

Do these before your trip, or your first hours will be frustrating. Each one links to a full guide:

1. **Sort your entry.** Many passports now get visa-free entry; others can use the [240-hour visa-free transit](/guides/china-240-hour-visa-free-transit). Coming from Hong Kong? Read the [border crossing guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen).
2. **Install an eSIM before you cross.** Google, WhatsApp, and Instagram don't work on Chinese networks without one (or a VPN). A [travel eSIM](/guides/best-esim-for-shenzhen) routes around this.
3. **Set up Alipay with your foreign card.** Almost everything here is paid by QR code, from restaurants to street snacks. [Setting it up](/guides/alipay-wechat-pay-setup) before you arrive saves a lot of awkward moments. (The metro now takes foreign cards at some gates; the [airport guide](/guides/shenzhen-airport-to-city) explains which ones.)
4. **Staying the night?** Here's how to [pick an area](/guides/where-to-stay-in-shenzhen).

## One Day: Pick One Area, Not Three

If you only have a day, the mistake is trying to see "Shenzhen". The city is big, and crossing it eats your day. Pick one area based on what you enjoy, and stay there.

| If you like… | Go to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shopping and eating | **Luohu** | Right next to the Hong Kong border, big malls within walking distance of each other |
| Walking, greenery, cafés | **OCT (Overseas Chinese Town)** | Lots of trees, pleasant to walk, restaurants and cafés with some atmosphere |
| Old streets, the sea, people-watching | **Shekou** | Older neighborhood, a lot of foreign residents, and you can walk to the water |

### Luohu: Shopping and Food, Right at the Border

If you're coming from Hong Kong for the day, this is the easiest choice, and it's where I usually take friends. A lot of Hong Kong people cross just to spend a day in Luohu now.

Once you're through the Luohu (Lo Wu) crossing, the malls are close. **Kingglory Plaza (金光华广场)** and the **Guomao** area are near enough to walk from the border, or it's one stop on Line 1 to Guomao station. **MixC Luohu (罗湖万象城)** is close to them too, so you can walk between them and spend the day eating and shopping without getting back on the metro.

**What you'll eat here:** Luohu is the old city, so the shops and restaurants are older than in the rest of Shenzhen. Hong Kong-style restaurants and Chaoshan beef hotpot are everywhere. It's also the part of town with the most Hong Kong visitors, which is worth knowing if you'd rather avoid the crowd than join it.

**If it rains,** stay here. Summer afternoons in Shenzhen come with sudden heavy rain, and Luohu has more malls packed together than anywhere else. Indoor days are easy in this city; that's part of what malls are for here.

If you want something cheaper and noisier, **Dongmen** (see the three-day plan below) is also on Line 1, a couple of stops further.

### OCT: The Area I Like Walking In

Overseas Chinese Town (华侨城) is one of my favorite places to walk. There are a lot of trees, it's comfortable on foot, and it's full of restaurants and cafés with some atmosphere.

Fewer Hong Kong day-trippers come out this far than to Luohu, so it's a quieter crowd.

A good starting point is **OCT-LOFT**, a former factory district turned into galleries, design studios, and cafés (Qiaocheng East station, Line 1). After that, just wander. This area doesn't need a plan; that's the point of it.

### Shekou: Old Streets and the Sea

Shekou is the other place I'd take a friend, and honestly the one I like most. It's good for walking, there's a lot to eat, much of it in older places that have been around for years, and you'll see far more foreign residents than elsewhere in Shenzhen.

**What you'll eat here:** Shekou has the highest concentration of foreign restaurants in the city, which is why expats have stayed in this area for decades. But the local food is good too, including claypot rice, which is worth ordering if you've never had it.

Walk down to the water at the **Sea World Culture and Arts Center** (海上世界文化艺术中心, home of Design Society), near Sea World station on Line 2. The building has long, wide staircases facing Shekou Bay. My favorite thing to do in Shekou is get a coffee and sit on those steps looking at the sea. The steps and public areas are free; exhibitions inside are ticketed.

There's also a newer mall further along the coast, **K11 ECOAST**. I wouldn't go out of your way for it on a one-day trip: it's awkward to get to and fairly quiet.

## Three Days: The Same Places, Plus a Few More

With three days you can do all three areas above without rushing, and add the things that don't fit in a single day.

### Day 1: Futian and Huaqiangbei

**Morning: Lianhuashan Park.** Walk up the small hill to the Deng Xiaoping statue at the top. It looks out over Futian's towers, and it's the best free view of what the city became.

**Afternoon: Huaqiangbei.** The electronics market district. Worth walking through even if you don't plan to buy anything. If you do, read the [Huaqiangbei guide](/guides/huaqiangbei-electronics-guide) first, and the [shopping guide](/guides/shopping-in-shenzhen) for how to bargain.

**Evening: Dongmen.** The down-to-earth side of Shenzhen: pedestrian streets and a lot of street snacks. The snacks are ordinary rather than special; you go for the atmosphere. The first McDonald's in mainland China opened here in 1990, and there's a small mall, **1234 Space**, sitting right above Laojie station.

### Day 2: Cycle the Coast to Shekou

This is my favorite day. The coastal greenway along **Shenzhen Bay** starts by the mangroves in Futian and runs west along the water all the way to Shekou. It's a long ride and a genuinely comfortable one, with Hong Kong across the bay.

**Where to start:** take Line 9 to **Shenzhen Bay Park station** and come out at **Exit D2**. You step out of the station and the water is right there, halfway along the mangroves, with bikes parked at the exit. Exit C also puts you by the sea if D2 is closed. From there, ride west.

**Not on a weekend morning or afternoon.** Bikes are barred from the park itself on weekends and public holidays for most of the daylight hours (reported as roughly 9am until early evening, the cut-off being later in summer than in winter). Weekdays are unrestricted. If you're here on a Saturday, do this ride early, late, or on another day.

There are shops along the way to buy water, but the gaps between them can be long, so fill up when you pass one rather than when you get thirsty.

**Getting a bike:** shared bikes are unlocked by scanning the QR code on the bike with Alipay or WeChat. That works for foreign visitors too, as long as you've linked a payment card and verified your identity in the app, which the [Alipay setup guide](/guides/alipay-wechat-pay-setup) walks through. Do it before the day, not while standing next to the bike.

Ride west, finish in Shekou, and spend the rest of the afternoon there as in the one-day plan above: the old streets, then coffee on the steps facing the sea.

If you don't want to cycle the whole thing, ride or walk a section and take the metro the rest of the way. Use the [metro](/guides/shenzhen-metro-guide) for the long hops and [DiDi](/guides/didi-shenzhen-guide) for the gaps.

### Day 3: OCT, Then Luohu

Spend the morning walking around **OCT**, then take Line 1 east to **Luohu** for an afternoon of malls and food. Both are on the same line. If you're heading back to Hong Kong, the Luohu crossing is right there at the end of the day.

### Crossing Back Without the Queue

Day trippers all go back at the same time, and the border is busy in the evening either way. Two ways to miss the worst of it:

- **Cross back around 6pm.** That's roughly when the Hong Kong crowd sits down to dinner in Luohu, so fewer of them are at the border.
- **Or eat early and cross right after.** If you linger after dinner, you'll be crossing with everyone else.

Either way, the principle is the same: do the thing everyone else is doing at a different hour. The [border guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen) has crossing hours and how long the checks take.

## What I'd Skip

- **Wutong Mountain.** I've climbed it. On a short trip I wouldn't: you'll be exhausted, and sore all over the next day.
- **Window of the World and Splendid China.** The big theme parks used to be the famous tourist stops. They don't have much buzz anymore, and I wouldn't go specifically for them.

## Practical Notes

- **Weather:** summers are hot, humid, and rainy; carry an umbrella.
- **Language:** less English than you'd expect. Translation apps work fine; save your hotel's name in Chinese on your phone.
- **Holidays:** avoid Chinese national holidays (Golden Week in early October, Spring Festival). Everywhere gets far more crowded.
