**The rebuilt Huanggang Port is scheduled to open at 06:30 on Monday 12 October 2026**, with passenger clearance available 24 hours a day. Hong Kong's Security Bureau and Shenzhen's Port Office announced the date jointly on 9 October. This replaces the earlier media-only target date. [Official opening announcement →](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202610/09/P2026100900280p.htm)

**Last checked 11 October 2026.** The opening is still ahead of us. This guide describes announced arrangements, not a visit to the new hall or a measured crossing time.

## The Switchover: Which Hall to Use

- **Before 06:30 on 12 October:** use the existing Lok Ma Chau road control point and Shenzhen's temporary Huanggang passenger hall.
- **From 06:30:** the new port is scheduled to take over passenger clearance. Passenger services between the old road control point and the temporary hall will be suspended.
- **If you arrive before the changeover:** officials say travellers already at the old facilities before 06:30 will be allowed to complete clearance there.

Allow extra time during the opening period. Hong Kong authorities warn that surrounding traffic may be busy while travellers adapt to the new arrangements. Read the [official switchover notice](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202610/09/P2026100900649.htm) before travelling.

**Do not confuse the two Lok Ma Chau crossings.** The suspended passenger service is at the **Lok Ma Chau road control point**, which serves Huanggang. **Lok Ma Chau Spur Line / Futian Checkpoint**, reached by the MTR East Rail Line, is a separately listed control point; the announcement is not a notice closing that rail crossing. Check the [Immigration Department's control-point directory](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/contactus/control_points.html) for its own hours.

## What Changes When It Opens

### One building instead of two

Until the announced switchover, crossing at Huanggang means clearing Hong Kong immigration at the road control point, travelling across, and clearing mainland immigration separately. The rebuilt crossing uses **co-location**: both sets of checks happen inside the same building, one after the other.

Hong Kong officials describe a target reduction from **roughly 30 minutes to about five**. That is an official expectation, not a measured average or a guarantee for passport holders using manual counters. Budget for queues. [Government explanation →](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/06/20260626/20260626_210634_044.html)

### You will be able to arrive by metro

This is the part that matters most for visitors, and it is easy to miss in the coverage.

For the new building, Shenzhen's Port Office lists **Huanggang Port station on Line 7**: Exit F leads to the North Plaza, and a **B2 underground connection** provides access to the port. Follow the official signs after the scheduled opening; this route has not been tested by this site's author. [Shenzhen's official port guide →](https://ka.sz.gov.cn/ztzl/hgkazt/index.html)

That changes the maths against Lo Wu and Futian, both of which have always won on exactly this point.

### Capacity built for a different scale

The crossing is designed for around **200,000 passenger trips a day**, and it stays open **24 hours** — it is the only Shenzhen–Hong Kong land crossing that does.

For travel around **APEC on 18-19 November**, see the [APEC visitor guide](/guides/apec-2026-shenzhen-visitor-guide); border access and summit registration are separate matters.

## Should You Change Which Crossing You Use?

**Choose by your onward journey, not just the new building.**

- **If you are coming from Hong Kong by MTR**, Lo Wu and Futian still make more sense today: you ride the East Rail line straight to the border. Huanggang has no MTR on the Hong Kong side, and that does not change when the new building opens.
- **If you are crossing overnight**, Huanggang remains the crossing to check for 24-hour passenger clearance. Confirm overnight transport as well as border hours.
- **If you are going to Futian or Huaqiangbei**, this is worth watching. The announced metro connection may make it more convenient, but queues and your Hong Kong-side transport still matter.

The [border crossing guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen) compares all six crossings as they work **now**, including hours, costs and which metro line each one drops you on.

## Hong Kong Transport: Check the New Arrangements

The new port has no direct MTR service on the Hong Kong side. Hong Kong's Transport Department has published bus, minibus, taxi and coach arrangements for its transport interchange. Some services are daytime-only or have limited operating windows, so **24-hour clearance does not mean every bus runs overnight**. The old Huanggang shuttle arrangement is being replaced as part of the switchover.

Use the [Transport Department's official Huanggang access page](https://www.td.gov.hk/en/transport_in_hong_kong/land_based_cross_boundary_transport/athp/) for your route, operating hours and operator contact details. Opening-day first services may differ from the normal timetable. This guide does not assume that an old airport-coach timetable carries over to the new port.

## What Still Needs a Real Visit

The announced facilities do not tell us actual queues, how easy the signs are to follow with luggage, or how long an unfamiliar passport holder will spend at manual inspection. I have not used the new hall. First-hand directions and photos should be added only after a real visit.

## Before You Travel

The date is now officially announced: **12 October 2026 at 06:30**, followed by 24-hour passenger service. On 11 October it remains a scheduled opening. Confirm your entry documents, check the official transport notices, and leave a buffer during the transition.
