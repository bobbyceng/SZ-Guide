Huanggang is the crossing most visitors ignore: no MTR on the Hong Kong side, a temporary passenger hall on the Shenzhen side, and a reputation for being the place you go at 3am because everything else is shut.

That is about to change. The rebuilt crossing is physically finished, and when it opens it will be the fastest way across the border for a large number of people.

What it does not have yet is an opening date.

**Last checked 27 September 2026.** This page is about a moving situation, so the date matters more than usual.

## What Is Confirmed

- **The Hong Kong port area legally exists.** Hong Kong's government gazetted the arrangement in July, and the Hong Kong Port Area inside the Shenzhen building came into being at 00:00 on **31 July 2026**, under Hong Kong jurisdiction.
- **The building works are done.** On **23 September** the surrounding road works passed completion inspection: ramps connecting to the Guangshen Expressway, surface roads, and two tunnels that separate taxis, buses and private cars. The North Plaza and the Lok Ma Chau bridge had been handed over earlier.
- **Hong Kong's 2026 Policy Address, published 16 September, says the crossing is expected to enter service in the fourth quarter** of this year.

In other words: finished, tested, waiting.

## What Is Not Confirmed: The Date

**No government on either side has announced an opening date.**

Hong Kong media reported in mid-September that both sides are working towards **Monday 12 October**, chosen to fall after the Golden Week holiday and on a weekday rather than a weekend. Several outlets carried the same target independently, but all of them attribute it to sources rather than an announcement.

Against that, on **20 September** Hong Kong's Secretary for Security said system testing had gone smoothly but still needed fine-tuning, and did not give a date.

**So: treat 12 October as a plausible target, not a fact.** If you are planning to cross at Huanggang in October, check the day before you travel. I will update this page when a date is actually announced.

## What Changes When It Opens

### One building instead of two

Today, crossing at Huanggang means clearing Hong Kong immigration at Lok Ma Chau, travelling across, and clearing mainland immigration separately. The rebuilt crossing uses **co-location**: both sets of checks happen inside the same building, one after the other.

Officials on both sides say this takes a crossing from **roughly 30 minutes down to about five**. That is the design target rather than a measured average — worth remembering the first time you see a queue.

### You will be able to arrive by metro

This is the part that matters most for visitors, and it is easy to miss in the coverage.

Shenzhen's **Line 7 has had a station called Huanggang Port since 2016**, but it has never connected usefully to the temporary hall. The North Plaza, already handed over, links that station to **level B2 of the new building** — so you should be able to step off the metro and walk into the clearance hall.

That changes the maths against Lo Wu and Futian, both of which have always won on exactly this point.

### Capacity built for a different scale

The crossing is designed for around **200,000 passenger trips a day**, and it stays open **24 hours** — it is the only Shenzhen–Hong Kong land crossing that does.

State media covering the completion of the road works also tied the project explicitly to cross-border traffic during **the APEC meeting in Shenzhen on 18-19 November**, which tells you something about the timeline everyone is working to.

## Should You Change Which Crossing You Use?

**Not yet, and not automatically.**

- **If you are coming from Hong Kong by MTR**, Lo Wu and Futian still make more sense today: you ride the East Rail line straight to the border. Huanggang has no MTR on the Hong Kong side, and that does not change when the new building opens.
- **If you are crossing overnight**, Huanggang is already your only option, and it is about to get much faster.
- **If you are going to Futian or Huaqiangbei**, this is worth watching. A five-minute crossing plus a direct metro connection would make it genuinely competitive with Futian Checkpoint.

The [border crossing guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen) compares all six crossings as they work **now**, including hours, costs and which metro line each one drops you on.

## What I Still Do Not Know

Being straight about the gaps, because they are the questions people will actually have:

- **What happens to the Lok Ma Chau control point and to Futian.** Co-location moves Hong Kong's checks into the Shenzhen building, but the official announcements do not say what that means for the existing Lok Ma Chau road crossing, or whether the Lok Ma Chau Spur Line to Futian Checkpoint is affected at all. **I have not found an official answer, and I am not going to guess at one.**
- **The Hong Kong bus routes.** Travel blogs have published lists of bus and minibus services to the new crossing. I have not been able to verify those against the Transport Department or the operators, so they are not in this guide.
- **What it is actually like.** I live in Shenzhen and cross at Luohu, not Huanggang. Everything above is from official announcements and press coverage, not from standing in that hall. **Once it opens, I will go, and this page will get the part no press release contains.**

## The Short Version

The building is finished. The road works passed inspection on 23 September. Hong Kong says fourth quarter; the press says 12 October; nobody official has said a date.

If it opens as reported, Shenzhen gets a 24-hour crossing that takes five minutes and connects straight to the metro — about a month before APEC brings the city a great deal of attention.
