**The rebuilt Huanggang Port opened at 06:30 on Monday 12 October 2026**, according to [RTHK's opening-day report](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/ch/component/k2/1873538-20261012.htm), published at 07:03. The report describes senior Hong Kong officials inspecting the operating port that morning; this is evidence of the actual opening, rather than just the earlier ceremony or scheduled date.

The [joint government announcement](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202610/09/P2026100900280p.htm) sets out **24-hour passenger clearance** and the replacement of the old passenger facilities.

**Last checked 12 October 2026.** This update uses official arrangements and opening-day reporting. I have not visited the new hall or measured a crossing time.

## Which Hall to Use Now

Use the **new Huanggang building**. The official switchover notice directs passenger clearance there from 06:30 on 12 October and suspends passenger services between the old Lok Ma Chau road control point and Shenzhen's temporary Huanggang hall. The provision allowing travellers who arrived before 06:30 to complete clearance at the old facilities was a changeover arrangement, not a reason to use the old hall now.

Allow extra time during the opening period. Hong Kong authorities warn that surrounding traffic may be busy while travellers adapt to the new arrangements. Read the [official switchover notice](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202610/09/P2026100900649.htm) before travelling.

**Do not confuse the two Lok Ma Chau crossings.** The suspended passenger service is at the **Lok Ma Chau road control point**, which serves Huanggang. **Lok Ma Chau Spur Line / Futian Checkpoint**, reached by the MTR East Rail Line, is a separately listed control point; the announcement is not a notice closing that rail crossing. Check the [Immigration Department's control-point directory](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/contactus/control_points.html) for its own hours.

## What Changes in the New Building

### One building instead of two

Before the switchover, crossing at Huanggang meant clearing Hong Kong immigration at the road control point, travelling across, and clearing mainland immigration separately. The rebuilt crossing uses **co-location**: both sets of checks happen inside the same building, one after the other.

Hong Kong officials describe a target reduction from **roughly 30 minutes to about five**. That is an official expectation, not a measured average or a guarantee for passport holders using manual counters. Budget for queues. [Government explanation →](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/06/20260626/20260626_210634_044.html)

### Shenzhen-side metro access

This is the part that matters most for visitors, and it is easy to miss in the coverage.

For the new building, Shenzhen's Port Office lists **Huanggang Port station on Line 7**: Exit F leads to the North Plaza, and a **B2 underground connection** provides access to the port. Follow the official signs; this route has not been tested by this site's author. [Shenzhen's official port guide →](https://ka.sz.gov.cn/ztzl/hgkazt/index.html)

That changes the maths against Lo Wu and Futian, both of which have always won on exactly this point.

### Capacity built for a different scale

The crossing is designed for around **200,000 passenger trips a day**, and it stays open **24 hours** — it is the only Shenzhen–Hong Kong land crossing that does.

For travel around **APEC on 18-19 November**, see the [APEC visitor guide](/guides/apec-2026-shenzhen-visitor-guide); border access and summit registration are separate matters.

## Should You Change Which Crossing You Use?

**Choose by your onward journey, not just the new building.**

- **If you are coming from Hong Kong by MTR**, Lo Wu and Futian still make more sense today: you ride the East Rail line straight to the border. Huanggang has no MTR on the Hong Kong side; the opening has not created a direct MTR service to it.
- **If you are crossing overnight**, Huanggang remains the crossing to check for 24-hour passenger clearance. Confirm overnight transport as well as border hours.
- **If you are going to Futian or Huaqiangbei**, this is worth watching. The announced metro connection may make it more convenient, but queues and your Hong Kong-side transport still matter.

The [border crossing guide](/guides/hong-kong-to-shenzhen) compares all six crossings as they work **now**, including hours, costs and which metro line each one drops you on.

## Hong Kong Transport: Check the New Arrangements

The new port has no direct MTR service on the Hong Kong side. Hong Kong's Transport Department has published bus, minibus, taxi and coach arrangements for its transport interchange. Some services are daytime-only or have limited operating windows, so **24-hour clearance does not mean every bus runs overnight**. The official switchover notice replaces the old Huanggang shuttle arrangement.

Use the [Transport Department's official Huanggang access page](https://www.td.gov.hk/en/transport_in_hong_kong/land_based_cross_boundary_transport/athp/) for your route, operating hours and operator contact details. Use the current regular timetable rather than the special first-service times for opening morning. This guide does not assume that an old airport-coach timetable carries over to the new port.

## What Still Needs a Real Visit

The announced facilities do not tell us actual queues, how easy the signs are to follow with luggage, or how long an unfamiliar passport holder will spend at manual inspection. I have not used the new hall. First-hand directions and photos should be added only after a real visit.

## Before You Travel

**The new port is open, with 24-hour passenger clearance in the official arrangements.** Confirm your entry documents, check current transport notices, and allow extra time while travellers and operators adapt. The opening report does not establish a guaranteed queue or crossing time.
